FULL DESCRIPTION  (2931 / 4000 characters)
----------------------------------------
You own a small metal fabrication shop. Work comes in off the board, steel moves across the floor, and every ticket has a date on it. Ship good parts on time and the phone keeps ringing. Ship late or sloppy and it stops.

CUT, BEND, WELD, SHIP
Every job routes itself through the machines it needs: the plasma table, the press brake, the MIG bay, the grind-and-paint booth, and, as the shop grows, the tube laser, the CNC mill and the powder coat line. Your hands are on the machines. Tap a running machine as the marker crosses the green band — nail the timing and the work flies, and the part comes out clean. Rush it and you can still ship, but the quality shows.

QUALITY IS THE WHOLE GAME
Every part carries a quality number, and that number sets your payout and your reputation. Reputation sets what the board pays you next. A shop that ships beautiful work gets better tickets; a shop that ships junk grinds away at its own margins.

HIRE THE FLOOR, LOSE THE TOUCH
Operators run a machine without you, day and night. They are also only ever adequate — parts they run alone drift toward merely acceptable. Automation buys you time and costs you margin, and deciding where to spend your own attention is the real management problem.

RUN THE BUSINESS
Rent, wages, and power come out every night whether you shipped or not. Upgrade machines for speed and more bays, add rack space for more jobs in flight, and unlock heavier work as your shop levels up — from weld brackets and gusset sets through handrails and stair stringers to trailer chassis and conveyor hoppers.

SELL UP AND OPEN A BIGGER SHOP
When you have taken a shop as far as it goes, sell it. You bank blueprints for what it earned, and everything else - cash, machines, crew, shop level - starts over in a bigger building. Blueprints buy permanent upgrades that carry across every shop you ever own: seed capital, a tooling library so machines start further up, a union hall that makes operators quicker, a trade school, and shop records that open the next place several levels in. Each relocation makes the climb faster than the last.

SOMETHING ALWAYS TURNS UP
A machine throws a fault mid-cut: pay the callout, or fix it yourself and lose it for two days while work piles up behind it. The steel price jumps overnight. An apprentice turns up wanting hours. A regular calls in a favour that no sensible shop would promise. Every one is a decision, and every one costs or pays.

THE SHOP RUNS WITHOUT YOU
Close the app and your operators keep working the jobs on the floor. Come back to finished work, parts shipped and the money banked. The calendar waits for you, so nothing goes late while you are gone - but a shop with nobody hired sits idle until you come back.

BUILT TO PLAY IN SHORT SHIFTS
A day takes under a minute. Progress saves itself. No accounts, no ads, no in-app purchases, no timers begging you to come back, and nothing to buy. Plays offline.
